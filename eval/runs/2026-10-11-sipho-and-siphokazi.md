- **1.1:** A small fishing harbour on a wild coast. Grey water, grey sky, a single row of forty-year-old wooden fishing-boats knocking against a concrete jetty.
- **1.2:** On the jetty, a salt-and-pepper-bearded fisherman in a sea-green oilskin coils a rope into a perfect figure-of-eight. This is SIPHO (50s). Gulls wheel. A dog trots past with a fish-head in its mouth. Sipho does not look up.
- **2.1:** A tin-roofed shack that smells of salt and diesel. Buckets of sardines on ice. A calendar from nineteen-ninety-something curls on the wall. SIPHOKAZI (17), in a school jersey two sizes too big, counts coins onto the counter. MAMA THEMBI (60s), the shop's owner, watches her over half-moon glasses.
- **2.2:** Your uncle knows you're here? My uncle knows everything. He just doesn't say it. Kazi. Is it true, what they say? The university letter came?
- **2.3:** Siphokazi nods. She cannot stop the smile. Cape Town. Marine science. I start in February.
- **2.4:** And Sipho? I haven't told him yet.
- **3.1:** An old white pick-up truck rattles down a dirt road toward the sea. Sipho drives. Siphokazi rides beside him with the bucket of sardines between her feet and a battered notebook on her knees: the TIDE BOOK. You're reading your grandmother's tide book again.
- **3.2:** She wrote down every tide for thirty-one years. Every storm. Every good catch. She wrote down every time I was late for supper.
- **3.3:** Siphokazi laughs. Sipho almost smiles. The bakkie hits a pothole and the sardines slosh. Kazi. Whatever you're not telling me, tell me on the boat.
- **4A.1:** The fishing boat NOMSA, blue paint peeling, rides long swells a mile offshore. Sipho hauls a line hand over hand. Siphokazi baits hooks with quick, practised fingers. On the horizon, a bruise-coloured bank of cloud is building, fast.
- **4A.2:** Neither of them sees it yet.
- **4B.1:** A cramped wheelhouse. A compass, a crackling VHF RADIO, a photograph of an old woman taped above the wheel. The radio bursts into life. All vessels, all vessels. Gale warning for the coast from Hole in the Wall to the river mouth. Return to harbour immediately.
- **4B.2:** Sipho is at the wheel in a heartbeat. Siphokazi scrambles in behind him. The tide's turning. If we go straight in over the bar now, we'll hit it on the ebb. I have crossed that bar a thousand times.
- **4B.3:** Not at half-past-four on a spring ebb in the teeth of a south-westerly. Gogo wrote it down. Look. She holds the tide book open under his nose. He does not look. Outside, a wave slaps over the bow. A coil of rope slides toward the open stern.
- **4B.4:** NO! THE ROPE! She lunges out of the cabin.
- **5.1:** Rain comes sideways. Siphokazi grabs the rope just before it goes over and hauls it back, soaked to the skin.
- **5.2:** Sipho brings the boat around, away from the harbour mouth, heading north along the coast.
- **6.1:** Siphokazi stumbles back in, dripping. Sipho does not take his eyes off the water. Where does the book say?
- **6.2:** The cove at Mngazana. Gogo sheltered there in eighty-seven. Then read me the way in. Every word.
- **7.1:** A sheltered bay under dark cliffs. The storm roars out at sea, but here the water is almost still. The Nomsa sits at anchor, a single lamp swinging in her wheelhouse.
- **7.2:** No one speaks. The rain eases. One by one, stars come out over the cliffs.
- **8.1:** Lamplight. Uncle and niece sit shoulder to shoulder on the floor, wrapped in one blanket, sharing a flask of sweet tea. When were you going to tell me? Tell you what?
- **8.2:** Mama Thembi has a loud voice and a small shop. A long pause. Siphokazi looks down at the tide book. I start in February. Marine science. I'm going to study all of this. The tides and the storms and the fish. Properly. Not just in a notebook.
- **8.3:** Your grandmother did it properly. In a notebook. I know. He takes the tide book from her, gently. He turns to the last page with writing on it. Then to the first empty page after it. He hands it back, and a pencil.
- **8.4:** Then you had better keep writing.
- **9.1:** Weeks later. A small kitchen, a paraffin lamp. A packed suitcase stands by the door.
- **9.2:** On the table, the tide book lies open beside a university prospectus. The newest entry in the book is in a young, neat hand.
- **9.3:** Nobody is in the room. The lamp flickers.
- **10.1:** The same grey water. The same row of boats. Sipho coils a rope into a perfect figure-of-eight.
