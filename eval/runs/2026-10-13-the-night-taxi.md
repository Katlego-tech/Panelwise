- **1.1:** Rows of white minibus taxis under orange floodlights. Engines idle. A vendor packs away a tray of oranges. BONGANI (50s), grey beard, flat cap, leans against a taxi with a dented front bumper. A string of plastic flowers hangs from its mirror. He counts coins into a tin. A boy in a too-big school blazer, THABO (9), stands alone by the queue barrier. He holds a crumpled piece of paper in both hands.
- **1.2:** You are waiting for somebody? My uncle. He said nine o'clock. Bongani checks his wristwatch. It is twenty past ten.
- **1.3:** Where does your uncle stay? Thabo holds up the paper. Bongani reads it under the floodlight. Orlando East. That is my last trip, as it happens.
- **1.4:** I don't have money for the taxi. Then tonight the taxi is free. Get in the front. The front is for important people.
- **2.1:** The city slides past the windows: shuttered shops, a burning drum with men warming their hands, a billboard for washing powder. Thabo sits in the front seat, seatbelt pulled tight across his chest. On the dashboard, a small radio glows green. And that was the last song before midnight. Drive safely out there, Soweto.
- **2.2:** What is in the bag? Thabo hugs a plastic shopping bag on his lap. A trophy. I came first in the spelling. My uncle didn't come to see.
- **2.3:** Spell something for me. Spell "roundabout". R, O, U, N, D, A, B, O, U, T. Hayi. I have been driving round them for thirty years and I can't spell it.
- **2.4:** The taxi swings around a roundabout. Thabo laughs.
- **3.1:** A row of small matchbox houses. Most windows are dark. A dog barks somewhere.
- **3.2:** The taxi stops outside a house with a green gate. The porch light is off.
- **4.1:** Thabo stares at the dark house. He doesn't move. Maybe he is sleeping.
- **4.2:** Then we wake him up. That is what uncles are for.
- **5.1:** Bongani knocks on the door. Nothing. He knocks again, harder. A light snaps on inside. The door opens on a chain. UNCLE SIZWE (30s), in a vest, squints out. It's past ten. Who is it?

- **5.5:** Bongani tips his cap and walks back to the taxi.
